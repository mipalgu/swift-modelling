# Refactoring plan: LegacyOrders 1.0 to Orders 2.0

## Findings

From `swift-ecore statistics`, `swift-ecore inspect` and the AQL queries
on the January export (`legacy/orders-2024-01.xmi`):

1. `Order` is the largest class by far: 15 features, 14 of them
   attributes that mix order, customer, address, payment, total and
   audit data.
2. `status` and `paymentMethod` are free text. The export spells the same
   status in different cases (`SHIPPED`, `Shipped`, `pending`).
3. Customer details are copied into every order. Five orders hold five
   different e-mail spellings for only four customers; ORD-2024-001 and
   ORD-2024-005 belong to the same customer.
4. Items name products by a code string. The code `cable-001` matches no
   product, so the item has no reliable link to the catalogue.
5. Totals are stored, not derived. ORD-2024-004 stores 949.98, but its
   items add up to 958.97.
6. `Product` mixes catalogue data with warehouse stock data, and names
   such as `qty` and `desc` are abbreviated.

## Goals

1. A `Customer` class with one object per e-mail address, referred to by
   its orders.
2. An `Address` class with street, suburb, state, postcode and country.
3. Enumerations `OrderStatus` and `PaymentMethod` instead of free text.
4. `OrderItem` refers to its `Product`; `qty` becomes `quantity`.
5. Totals are computed from the items when needed, never stored.
6. Stock data moves to a `StockLevel` object owned by its product.
7. The business rules are OCL invariants of the metamodel, so that
   `swift-ecore validate` checks every migrated model.

## Pipeline

1. Clean each legacy export (a refining ATL transformation): normalise
   the case of statuses, payment methods, e-mail addresses and product
   codes.
2. Migrate the cleaned export to Orders 2.0 (an ATL transformation).
3. Validate the result against `Orders.ecore`, including its invariants.

## Success criteria

- Every migrated model validates against `Orders.ecore`, also with
  `--strict`.
- Orders, items and products keep their numbers; there is one customer
  per distinct e-mail address.
- Every item refers to a product, and every order keeps its status.
- Writing a migrated model back in the legacy format reproduces the
  cleaned export, except for wrong stored totals, addresses without a
  country and the dropped `lastModified` attribute.
- An XMI to JSON to XMI round trip of each migrated model reports no
  differences.
